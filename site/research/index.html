Research and experiments

What I measured, and what it does not prove

Selected findings from building governed AI systems. Each one is labelled for what it actually is, and each states its own limits. Several did not go the way I expected, and they are here because of that.

LabelMeaning on this page
Measured findingA number from a real run, with the method and sample stated
Experiment / prototypeBuilt and run, but not yet evaluated at a scale that supports a general claim
Architectural proposalA design I have argued for and partly built, not yet proven
HypothesisWhat I currently believe and am testing, and could be wrong about

Unless stated otherwise, every result below comes from my own systems on a single Apple M1 machine with 16 GB of memory, and much of it uses synthetic data. None of it is a benchmark of anyone else's product.

  • Measured finding
  • AI security

Detection is the weakest control, so do not build on it

Question. How much protection does a heuristic prompt-injection detector really provide?

Method. I wrote a corpus of attack phrasings, tuned a deny-list detector on some of them, and held out paraphrases of the same attacks.

Result. It caught 54 of 54 phrasings it was tuned on and 1 of 27 held-out paraphrases.

So what. This finding changed the architecture. Jarvis rests on structural controls (capabilities that do not exist, processes that cannot reach each other) and its strongest tests assert that untrusted content changes nothing even when the detector stays silent.

Limits: one detector, one author's corpus. The finding is about heuristic detection in general, not about any vendor's product.

A local memory engine against acceptance criteria set in advance

Question. Is an on-device extraction and retrieval pipeline good enough to build on, without sending a personal corpus to a cloud service?

Method. I wrote the acceptance criteria before the run, with a pre-agreed fallback plan if it failed. The pipeline was then scored on a deterministic synthetic corpus of 630 events, 450 seeded facts and 570 probes, beside a control run.

The history matters as much as the table. This was the fourth measurement. The third failed three criteria and sent the architecture back for reconsideration; this one passed every criterion a machine can decide. The final go or no-go decision is mine, and it is still open.

Limits: synthetic, templated data measures containment rather than semantic understanding. One corpus, one configuration, and latency excludes the answering model.

Hybrid retrieval did not beat plain vector search

I chose a hybrid design (keyword and vector search fused by reciprocal rank fusion) partly expecting it to win. On recall@20 over 541 memories and 18 seeded questions, keyword search found 12, vector search 17 and the fused result 16. I published that result, and my own write-up says plainly that it does not prove hybrid beats a strong semantic retriever on this corpus.

Limits: 18 questions is a small sample. The point is the discipline of reporting it, not the ranking.

Ask a small model what something is, not whether it is allowed

Observation. A small on-device model asked whether live memory records (mostly derived from my own media library) could be shared refused to decide on most of them: 254 of 300 in a pilot.

Experiment. Holding the model and weights fixed, a pre-registered 2×2 prompt experiment on fresh samples changed only the question. Asking what a record is, rather than whether it may leave, cut abstentions from 136 of 150 to 0 of 150.

So what. The model could categorise all along. It was declining to adjudicate, and that reluctance had been quietly acting as a safety guard. The replacement design removes all approval authority from the model: it describes, and a deterministic rule over a human-approved list decides. It is built and awaiting my sign-off before it is switched on.

Limits: one model, one fairly uniform corpus; the held-out confirmatory run is still to come.

  • Measured finding
  • Assurance method

Pre-registered adversarial tests that become permanent

For a data-sharing policy I registered 32 adversarial cases and their expected outcomes in version control before the first run, including benign controls so that a rule refusing everything could not pass. The harness collects every deviation before failing, rather than stopping at the first. Result: 0 deviations out of 32, and a byte-for-byte check that nothing was written. The suite now runs on every build.

Limits: by its own account this proves the mechanism, not that the underlying list is complete.

  • Measured finding
  • Retrieval design

Evidence tiers are kinds, not scores

For cross-modal search (linking audio, video and images), I separated results into three tiers: verified (a standing claim says so), inferred (a named chain of links implies it) and similar (vectors only). A similarity score, however high, can never promote a result to verified, and a test fails if a new route to "verified" is added. On a nine-case gold set, precision and recall were 1.0 in both deterministic and semantic configurations. The deterministic path answered in a median of 227 µs, against 3.3 s with semantic models.

Two lessons came from the real corpus. Sorting a mixed result list by score let the most numerous kind crowd out the rest, so results are now interleaved by kind within each tier. And a gold set curated under one configuration and scored under another read 0.417 with nothing actually wrong, so gold cases now declare their configuration.

Limits: my own media library on one machine; some relationship types exist only in test fixtures so far.

  • AI FinOps

Cost-aware model routing, measured rather than modelled

A deterministic router (no model in the decision) picks the least expensive model expected to clear each task's quality and risk bar, and escalates with a named human's approval. Two separately authorised live runs produced same-token cost reductions of 39.6% and 40.4% against a deliberately cheapest-possible frontier baseline, for a total spend of about US$0.02. A separate offline process can re-price the evidence from raw usage without spending anything.

Limits: stated first in the original write-up. No quality claim survives in this evidence, the baseline assumes the same token counts, and no invoice was reconciled. Two cents proves the apparatus, not a business case.

  • Measured finding

A shadow decision plane: AI judges that advise but cannot decide

Proposal. Run an external AI judge beside a deterministic policy, record where they disagree, and never let the judge's answer replace the policy's. If the advisory answer is ever substituted for the real decision, the mission fails.

Findings along the way. An apparent contradiction (policy said "owner approval required", the judge said 0.54) disappeared once I read the service contract. The value is a probability of "yes", calibrated only in aggregate, so 0.54 means no signal, not "54% sure". I also found that a configured 300 ms timeout took over 6 seconds against a simulated rate limit, because it applied per attempt. The lesson: own your deadline instead of trusting a client library's.

Limits: very few live comparisons so far, so no accuracy claim is made.

Explanations checked against the evidence after they are written

A hosted model writes a plain-language explanation of a mission or a memory. The explanation is then checked against the recorded evidence before it is shown. A count guard is designed to withhold an explanation whose numbers contradict the evidence. One live acceptance run cost US$0.018 against a US$0.05 cap.

Limits: owner acceptance testing only, not a user study.

A Copilot Studio agent for portfolio prioritisation

Two published Copilot Studio agents with Entra ID sign-in capture a work request and its Weighted Shortest Job First (WSJF) inputs in conversation, then hand them to a Power Automate agent flow that records the item. Version one wrote to a SharePoint list. Version two moved to a custom Dataverse table with typed columns, which is the design iteration I would recommend to anyone starting with a list.

Limits: a three-day proof on a single-user developer tenant, with no solution-based lifecycle management.

  • Measured finding
  • Engineering practice

Two green test suites can hide two code paths that disagree

One rule had two write paths, each with its own passing test suite, and one path silently dropped a bad argument. The fix was a single shared decision table plus one test asserting that both paths agree across the whole input matrix, itself checked by deliberately breaking the code. A related finding: a frame-rate benchmark that read a flat 30.0 when idle and 20.0 under load was measuring the test harness, not the renderer. A same-minute A/B against the old version confirmed there was no regression.

  • Hypothesis
  • Agentic AI governance

Self-improvement scales by widening evidence, not authority

I believe most of the value of an AI system that improves itself can be captured at the prepare level: finding, ranking, specifying and drafting work. Execution authority can stay narrow, scoped and human-signed without making the loop impractical. The Jarvis lifecycle is my test of that belief. So far its core path has run once, on a deliberately small change, so this remains a hypothesis rather than a finding.
